Packing Holiday Decorations So Next Year Is Easy

November 25, 2026

← All Posts

The week after the holidays, decorations come down faster than they went up. Lights get coiled, ornaments go back in whatever box is closest, and the wrapping paper gets stuffed in a bag on top. In January that feels like a win. Next November, you're opening six boxes to find the one with the tree lights.

The fix is not a more complicated storage system. It's labeling the box so you can read what's inside before you pick it up. TAG-A-ROOM box content labels are made for that. They let you say what is inside before the tape goes on.

Shop box content labels

This post is about packing holiday decorations for storage at home, in the attic, the garage, or a closet, so next year's setup is easier. If you're labeling boxes that are going into a storage unit for a move or a longer hold, we already have a page on color-coded labels for storage units.

Why a marker on the tape is not enough

A lot of decoration boxes already have a word on them. "Xmas." "Lights." "Kitchen." That was enough the year you packed them, when you still remembered that "Lights" meant the outdoor icicle lights, not the tree lights, and not the spare bulbs in a bag at the bottom.

A year later, that one word is a guess. You pull the box down, open it, and put it back. Then you do it again. Fragile ornaments take the most damage in that shuffle, because they get opened just to find out they were the wrong box.

A box content label gives you room to be specific. "Indoor tree lights, 2 strands, plus spare bulbs." "Glass ornaments, red and gold, packing paper inside." "Wrapping paper, ribbon, and gift bags." That's the difference between a box you have to open and a box you can leave on the shelf.

Write it before the tape goes on. Once the box is sealed and stacked, you will not go back and add a better label.

What to write on box content labels

Keep it short, and make it useful to tired-you next November. A good holiday decoration label usually has three things:

  • What it is. Ornaments, tree lights, outdoor lights, stockings, wrapping, holiday dishes, the tree stand, the wreath hooks.
  • Where it belongs. Indoor tree, front porch, dining table, kids' rooms.
  • Anything fragile or annoying to rediscover. Glass, ceramic, a tangled light strand, a bag of hooks, the extra bulbs.

You do not need a novel. You need enough that you can leave the box closed if it's the wrong one.

A few examples that work well on box content labels:

  • "Indoor tree: lights, 3 strands, warm white"
  • "Glass ornaments, shatter-prone, tissue packed"
  • "Outdoor icicle lights, front eaves, extension cord in box"
  • "Holiday dishes and serving platters, kitchen"
  • "Wrapping paper, ribbon, tissue, gift bags"
  • "Stockings, mantle hooks, and the tree skirt"
  • "Outdoor wreath, door hanger, and spare bows"

If a box is mixed, say so. "Tree skirt, stocking holders, and leftover ribbon" is better than "Misc. Xmas," which is how boxes get opened every year.

Number the boxes if you have more than a few. "Holiday 3 of 8: indoor lights" is easier to hunt for than eight boxes that all say "Christmas." Keep a simple list on your phone or a paper in the first box you will grab next year.

Group decorations by how you'll unpack them, not by how you took them down

Take-down order is usually "whatever is closest." Next year's setup is not. You'll want the tree and lights first, then ornaments, then the kitchen and table, then the outdoor decorations, then the wrapping, which you may not need until later.

Pack in that order if you can, and label in that order even if you can't. A stack that reads, from the front: tree stand and lights, ornaments, kitchen, outdoor, wrapping, is a stack you can work through without unstacking the whole closet.

That's also why one generic "holiday" box is a problem. The wrapping paper does not need to live with the glass ornaments. The outdoor extension cords do not need to live with the stocking holders. Split them, then use the box content label to say which is which.

If you keep decorations in more than one place, say that on the label too. "Garage shelf, left side, outdoor lights" saves a trip to the attic with the wrong box.

Add color so you can find the right stack

Box content labels tell you what's inside. Color coding labels help you see the group from across the garage.

Give each holiday category one color and stick to it. One color for tree and indoor lights. One for ornaments and breakables. One for kitchen and table. One for outdoor decorations. One for wrapping and gift supplies. You don't need a dozen colors. You need a set you can recognize without squinting.

Bright room colors your crew can read from across the house work the same way in a garage or attic: you can tell the outdoor-lights stack from the ornament stack before you start climbing.

If you already use TAG-A-ROOM colors for a move, don't reuse those colors for decorations unless you'll remember the difference. A simple legend, a note on your phone or a card on the shelf, is enough. "Blue: indoor tree. Green: outdoor. Red: ornaments. Yellow: wrapping."

Color does not replace the box content label. Color gets you to the right stack. The content label tells you whether you can leave the box closed.

Where the labels go, and what not to claim

Put labels on the side of the box, not only on the top. Boxes get stacked, and a top-only label disappears. If a box might sit on its end, add a second label on another side.

Don't tape over the label with packing tape if you can help it. You want to read it next year.

We don't claim these labels come off residue-free, and we don't promise they'll peel away easily. Room labels are made to stay put. That's the point on a box that may sit in a garage or attic until next winter. If you need a label for a bin you already use every year, put the content label on the bin where you can see it, and leave it there.

A simple packing order for the week after the holidays

If the decorations are still up, or they're in a pile on the floor, this order keeps the labeling honest:

  1. Start with the fragile stuff. Ornaments, glass, ceramic village pieces. Pack them so they don't rattle, then write a specific box content label before the tape goes on.
  2. Lights next, coiled and sorted. Indoor and outdoor in separate boxes. Put spare bulbs and the extension cords in the box they'll actually be used with, and say so on the label.
  3. Tree parts. Stand, skirt, toppers, extra hooks. These are the first things you'll want next year, so don't bury them under wrapping paper.
  4. Kitchen and table. Dishes, runners, candle holders. One category, one set of labels.
  5. Outdoor decorations. Wreaths, lawn pieces, hooks, timers. These often live in the garage already. Label them as if you won't see them for eleven months.
  6. Wrapping last. Paper, ribbon, bags, and leftover tags. This box can sit behind the others. Just make sure the label says wrapping, not "holiday," or you'll open it in early November looking for the tree stand.

If you're packing in a hurry, do the labels anyway. A specific content label on a slightly messy box is still better than a tidy box that says "Xmas."

What this looks like next November

Next year, you should be able to walk to the shelf and pull only what you need that day. Tree and lights on a Saturday. Ornaments once the tree is up. Outdoor decorations when the weather is decent. Wrapping when you actually start gifts.

You should not have to open a box to find out what's in it. That's the whole test. If you still have to open boxes to identify them, the label wasn't specific enough, and that's a fix you can make the following January.

The same system works if you move mid-year and the holiday boxes go on a truck. The content label still tells you what's inside, and the color still tells you which stack is which. You don't have to rebuild the plan from scratch.

FAQ

What should I write on holiday decoration boxes?

Use box content labels and be specific: what's inside, where it belongs, and whether it's fragile. "Indoor tree lights, 2 strands, spare bulbs" is better than "Lights."

Should I use color coding too?

Yes, if you have more than a few boxes. Color coding labels mark the category so you can find the right stack. The content label still does the work of telling you what's inside.

Where should I put the labels?

On the sides of the box, not only the top, so you can read them when boxes are stacked.

Do the labels come off easily?

We don't claim residue-free or easy removal. These labels are meant to stay on the box until you need them next year.

Is this the same as your storage unit page?

No. That page is about labeling boxes going into a storage unit. This post is about packing holiday decorations after the season so next year's setup is easier.
